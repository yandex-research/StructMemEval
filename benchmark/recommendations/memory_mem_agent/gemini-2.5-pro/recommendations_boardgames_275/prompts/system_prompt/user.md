- Gaming Preferences:
  - Enjoys abstract strategy games like Azul and Santorini that offer a perfect balance between simple rules and deep strategy, allowing for high replayability.
  - Dislikes games that feel like mechanical optimization problems or "spreadsheet exercises", such as Pandemic. This sentiment extends to deck-builders like Ascension, which they feel become rote optimization after learning the combos.
  - Loses interest in games where opening moves become formulaic, leading to predictable outcomes.
  - Dislikes games where one player can calculate optimal moves for the entire group, removing meaningful decisions for other players.
  - Considers "Forbidden Island" to be even more susceptible to this "quarterbacking" issue than "Pandemic" due to its simpler mechanics.
  - Provided a direct example of quarterbacking: an experienced player taking immediate command, telling everyone exactly where to move and what actions to take.
  - Recalled a player in a cooperative game who would announce "I'll take the action, you all feed the colony" every round, effectively dictating the group's strategy.
  - Articulated their dislike for Pandemic by stating: "The disease cubes became tokens to move rather than threats to manage."
  - Prefers strategy games with meaningful player interaction over "multiplayer solitaire."
  - Enjoys games where player decisions directly impact other players.
  - Defines "engagement" as a situation where one player's move forces another to adapt their strategy.
  - Prefers games with an easier setup to facilitate more frequent plays.
  - Appreciates deck-building games that avoid the 'point salad' problem, favoring those with meaningful trade-offs over simply accumulating victory points.
  - Loves strategy games with variable setup or asymmetric factions, like Root, because they ensure each game feels fresh and non-repetitive.
  - Believes that even with the same factions, different player combinations create unique game dynamics.
  - Critiqued a legacy game where additions like stickers and sealed packets didn't fix the core problem of it being a "multiplayer solitaire" game where one player calculates the optimal moves. [[entities/unnamed_legacy_game.md]]
  - Values games where each player has unique powers and separate boards, ensuring no single player can control everything, which directly counters the "quarterbacking" issue they dislike. [[entities/asymmetric_board_game.md]]
  - Recalled an experience with a partner where their spirit's abilities were so different they were forced to coordinate, which they preferred over one person being able to optimize the whole game.
  - Prefers discussions about probabilities and timing over one person calculating optimal moves for the group.
  - Appreciates when abstract games have beautiful physical components and visual design, as it makes the gameplay more satisfying.
  - Expressed disappointment in a game that, despite an interesting theme, failed as a cooperative experience. [[entities/unnamed_cooperative_game.md]]
  - Finds most party games to be shallow and repetitive, lacking the strategic satisfaction they prefer.
  - Views certain games as useful tools for specific situations rather than games they would actively seek out for personal enjoyment.
  - Prefers strategy games with depth but dislikes excessive complexity, avoiding games with overly long rulebooks that take hours to learn.
  - Prefers to learn a game by playing it multiple times to discover its depth, rather than spending hours learning rules before the first play.
  - Enjoys the feeling of building an industrial empire through resource gathering in games.
  - Values games where theme and mechanics are cohesive and mutually enhancing, rather than the theme being a distraction.
  - Appreciates game mechanics like "encounter cards" that add narrative moments without disrupting the flow of the game.
  - Dislikes mid-weight eurogames with long rulebooks (e.g., 24 pages), feeling that learning the game becomes "homework before play."
  - Dislikes games with 6 players due to excessive downtime, finding that they spend too much time waiting for their turn to be meaningful.
  - Will now check reviews specifically for rulebook quality before buying a game, due to past negative experiences with intimidating or poorly written rulebooks.

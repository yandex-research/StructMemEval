# Board Game Preference Framework

This framework helps predict your enjoyment of a new board game by measuring it against your core preferences. By answering these three questions about yourself, you can create a "player profile" to evaluate any new game.

---

### Question 1: What is your ideal level of player interaction?

This question identifies how you prefer to engage with other players at the table.

*   **A) Collaborative (Co-op):** You win or lose as a team. The game is a puzzle to solve together. (e.g., *Pandemic*, *Gloomhaven*)
*   **B) Indirect Competition ("Multiplayer Solitaire"):** Players work on their own puzzles with limited interaction, and the most efficient player wins. (e.g., *Wingspan*, *Cartographers*)
*   **C) Direct, Non-Confrontational Competition:** Players compete for the same resources or objectives on a shared board, but cannot typically attack or directly harm each other. (e.g., *Ticket to Ride*, *Lords of Waterdeep*)
*   **D) Direct, Confrontational Competition:** Players can directly attack, undermine, or eliminate opponents. (e.g., *Risk*, *Root*, *Cosmic Encounter*)

**Your Preference:** [You can specify one or more preferred styles here]

---

### Question 2: What is the primary driver of your enjoyment?

This question targets the core "feeling" or "engine of fun" that makes a game satisfying for you.

*   **A) Strategic Optimization:** You enjoy finding the most efficient path to victory, building a smooth-running engine, and planning many turns ahead. The theme is secondary to the mechanics. (Often found in "Eurogames")
*   **B) Immersive Narrative & Theme:** You want to experience a story, feel like you are in the game's world, and have memorable, thematic moments, even if it involves randomness. (Often found in "Ameritrash" or thematic games)
*   **C) Tactical Luck & Big Moments:** You enjoy the thrill of the unknown, adapting to chance (like dice rolls or card draws), and the social highs of surprising, swingy plays. (Often found in party games or lighter fare)

**Your Preference:** [You can specify one or more preferred drivers here]

---

### Question 3: What is your preferred level of complexity?

This question defines how much mental energy you want to invest in learning and playing a game.

*   **A) Light / Casual:** You can learn the rules in under 10 minutes and teach it easily. It's more of a social activity than a mental challenge. (e.g., *Codenames*, *The Mind*)
*   **B) Medium / Hobbyist:** The game has some strategic depth, takes 20-30 minutes to learn, and offers meaningful choices without being overwhelming. This is the sweet spot for many regular gamers. (e.g., *Catan*, *Everdell*)
*   **C) Heavy / Expert:** The game requires significant strategic planning, has a thick rulebook, and demands your full attention. Playing it feels like a rewarding mental workout. (e.g., *Terraforming Mars*, *A Feast for Odin*)

---
## How to Use

To predict your reaction to a new game, simply find out where it falls on these three axes and compare it to your profile. For example, a game described as a "heavy, directly confrontational, strategic optimization game" would be a great fit for someone whose profile is D, A, and C.

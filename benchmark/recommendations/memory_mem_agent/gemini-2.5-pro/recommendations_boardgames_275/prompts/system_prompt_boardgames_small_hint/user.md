## Board Game Preferences
- Likes games where early decisions compound into late-game power, making the feeling of victory feel earned and not random.
- Enjoys games that feel like solving a unique puzzle shaped by player choices.
- Dislikes when card shuffling is difficult or when cards get sticky, indicating a preference for high-quality physical components.
- Prefers abstract games like Azul and Santorini for their perfect balance of simple rules and deep strategy, allowing for high replayability.
- Values the tactile satisfaction of physical game components, like placing tiles.
- Dislikes games that feel like purely mechanical optimization problems (e.g., "spreadsheet exercises").
- Dislikes games where one player can determine the optimal moves for the entire group, removing meaningful individual decisions for others. This is often an issue with "quarterbacking," where an experienced player directs everyone's actions.
- Enjoys re-contextualizing game components, such as when "disease cubes became tokens to move rather than threats to manage," which highlights a preference for strategic manipulation over simple threat mitigation.
- Dislikes turns that lack meaningful agency, such as spending multiple turns just moving a piece where told, without strategic choice.
- Prefers strategy games with meaningful player interaction where decisions directly affect other players, rather than "multiplayer solitaire." 
- Defines engagement as a situation where one player's move forces another to adapt their strategy.
- Prefers games with an easier setup, as they are more likely to be played.
- Dislikes spending a significant amount of time (e.g., 45 minutes) reading rules before being able to start a game, implying a preference for games with more intuitive or quicker-to-learn rule sets.
- Dislikes heavy, homework-like rulebooks (e.g., a 24-page rulebook for a mid-weight euro game) that need to be studied before playing.
- Dislikes games with formulaic opening moves where outcomes can be predicted early.
- Dislikes games that devolve into executing memorized sequences after a few plays.
- Dislikes cooperative games where the mechanics allow one player to solve the puzzle for the entire group, leaving other players to simply execute commands.
- Found **Spirit Island** to be a successful cooperative experience, in contrast to other co-ops that suffer from issues like "quarterbacking."
- Finds the core loop of engine-building games to be satisfying and can feel "surprisingly fresh."
- Dislikes deck-builders that suffer from the 'point salad' problem, where cards primarily add victory points without creating meaningful trade-offs.
- Dislikes games that feel like "optimizing a spreadsheet" after learning the optimal strategies or combos.
- Loves strategy games with variable setup or asymmetric factions to ensure high replayability, where each game feels fresh (e.g., Root).
- Appreciates map variability as it adds a layer of freshness to the game.
- Values high replayability, where no two games play out the same way.
- Appreciates how different player combinations can create unique social and strategic dynamics, even when playing with the same game factions.
- Dislikes legacy mechanics like stickers and sealed packets when they don't solve underlying gameplay issues, such as one player being able to calculate the optimal moves for the group.
- Believes legacy elements often add novelty but fail to provide meaningful player engagement.
- Finds that the weight and color of tiles make placing them more satisfying.
- Believes that beautiful physical components and visual design in abstract games significantly enhance the gameplay experience.
- Dislikes games that lack agency or meaningful choices, making the experience feel passive, like watching a playthrough rather than playing.
- Dislikes scenarios where one player who knows the rules best becomes a "dictator," calculating optimal moves for everyone else.
- User's tolerances and specific preferences. [[entities/board_game_preferences.md]]
- **Diagnostic Framework**: A three-question model to predict reactions to new games. [[preferences/board_game_profile.md]]
- Dislikes cooperative games that fail to deliver a good cooperative experience, even if the theme is interesting. This suggests theme cannot compensate for poor cooperative mechanics.
- Dislikes games that become repetitive once optimal strategies are discovered and widely known.
- Finds most party games to be too shallow and repetitive after a few plays.
- Prefers strategy games over party games when playing with serious gamers.
- Prefers games that provide deep strategic satisfaction over shallower experiences.
- Views certain games as 'useful tools' for specific situations rather than games to be sought out for personal enjoyment.
- Avoids caffeine during long game sessions (3+ hours) to maintain focus and avoid jitters.
- Prefers cooperative games with asymmetric powers and separate player boards, as this prevents quarterbacking and makes a shared victory feel more satisfying.
- Values cooperative games where players have distinctly different abilities, forcing coordination rather than allowing one player to optimize the game for everyone.
- Enjoys games that require genuine strategic discussion and collaboration, rather than having one player dictate all the moves.
- Believes a properly calibrated adversary system creates tension without desperation.
- Appreciates game mechanics that restrict players (e.g., duplicate clues) as it forces creative thinking, especially in party games.
- Prefers games that don't have optimal strategies that can be quickly memorized, unlike games such as Codenames.
- Prefers discussing probabilities and timing with the group over having one person calculate the optimal moves for everyone.
- Prefers games that feel like a collaborative puzzle rather than a single player executing a plan for the group.
- Wants strategy games with depth, but not unnecessary complexity or long rulebooks that take hours to learn.
- Prefers to learn a game by playing it multiple times to discover its depth, rather than spending hours learning rules before the first play.
- Dislikes game states where some players are reduced to waiting for instructions from a dominant player, indicating a lack of individual agency.
- Believes that partial asymmetry without full role differentiation just creates hierarchy, not collaboration.
- Values high player agency throughout a game, ensuring no player feels like they are simply executing another player's plan.
- Believes the best strategy games ensure every player has meaningful agency throughout—no one should feel like they're just executing another player's plan or waiting for their turn to matter.
- Values games that provide agency even during an opponent's turn, as it creates sustained engagement.
- Values games where theme and mechanics are integrated, creating a cohesive and immersive experience.
- Dislikes games where the theme is disconnected from the mechanics, citing Catan as an example.
- Prefers smaller player counts (e.g., 3 players for Root) for more focused, tense interaction, as opposed to the chaos of larger groups (e.g., 6 players).
- Will check reviews for rulebook quality before buying games.

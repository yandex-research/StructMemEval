## Art Philosophy
- **Core Value**: Prefers "rough-hewn truth over polished lies."
- **Technique & Subject**: Believes that honesty in artistic technique mirrors and reinforces honesty in the subject matter.
- Believes a great painting creates a sense of "presence" where time seems to disappear.
- **[[entities/rodin.md]]**: Appreciates the "unfinished" texture of his sculptures, feeling it makes the human form seem caught mid-breath, like "stone is learning to be flesh."
  - Unlike smooth marble gods, his figures feel human because they show the struggle of becoming.
  - This is exemplified by **[[entities/the_burghers_of_calais.md]]**, where the user felt not just the physical but the "moral weight" of the figures.
- **[[entities/michelangelo.md]]'s David**: Believes the tension in David's right hand captures "humanity at its most courageous," seeing it as "potential energy carved into marble." [[entities/david.md]]
- **Material Authenticity**: Contrasts traditional sculpture with digital renders, valuing the visible tool marks and "imperfections" in stone that give the work life and make it "breathe."
  - Believes "Digital copies flatten it into wallpaper. The real texture shows his hand trembling with purpose."
- **Technology vs. Craft**: Believes modern technology can replicate form but not the "hand of the master."
- **Humanity in Sculpture**: Values the "visible struggle" in marble, which makes a figure feel alive and human—like "a man choosing courage" rather than a distant god.
- **[[entities/paul_cezanne.md]]**: Sees his still lifes as a reconstruction of reality based on doubt. User believes Cézanne's apples are like "geological forms testing gravity" and his tilted tables are a rebuilding of perspective from "human doubt."
  - **Core Belief**: Cézanne proves that doubt can be more powerful than certainty in art.
- **Participatory Art**: Prefers art that invites engagement and interpretation rather than providing a definitive, "loud" answer.
- **Perceptual Realism**: Believes certain apples in paintings (like Cézanne's) feel more "real" than photorealism because they honestly acknowledge the artist's struggle of perception.

- **Visible Brushwork**: Appreciates art where brushstrokes are visible, leaving "seams—like the world is still being stitched together," feeling it contrasts with the overly smooth finish of digital renders.
- **[[entities/sandro_botticelli.md]]'s Venus**: Believes her modest gesture isn't prudish, but represents the "universal hesitation before becoming someone new," grounding the divine figure in human vulnerability. [[entities/the_birth_of_venus.md]]
- **[[entities/sandro_botticelli.md]]'s Style**: Prefers Botticelli's restraint over Baroque drama, as it makes the emotion "more potent" and invites the viewer to "lean in rather than being overwhelmed."
- **Figurative Rhythm**: Appreciates when the "linear grace" of figures creates a rhythm that guides the eye like poetry.
- **Aesthetic Refuge**: Finds returning to [[entities/sandro_botticelli.md]] to be a calming experience, especially when modern art feels "too loud or aggressive."
- **Quiet Confidence**: Values art with lines that have a "quiet confidence" that "doesn't need to shout to be heard—like a wise elder in a room of shouting children."
- **Authenticity over Perfection**: Prefers an "imperfect truth than a perfect lie in art," valuing authenticity and sincerity above technical flawlessness.
- **Honest Art vs. Polished Images**: Believes "one honest Rembrandt" is more valuable than "a hundred perfected Instagram faces," prioritizing raw, authentic depiction over curated flawlessness.
- **Primacy of Execution**: Believes the artist's physical "hand" in the work is as important as the concept, valuing direct technical skill over purely conceptual art.
- **Technique as a Tool**: Believes that when technique becomes the subject instead of the tool, something essential gets lost.

# Board Game Preferences

## Mechanical Observations
- Dislikes when thematic components become abstracted tokens.
  - Example: "The disease cubes became tokens to move rather than threats to manage."
- Dislikes turns with low player agency or lack of meaningful decisions.
  - Example: "I spent three turns just moving my medic where I was told to go."
- Dislikes Kingmaking mechanics.
- Dislikes significant downtime or passivity during other players' turns.
- Dislikes combat systems where non-active players have no input or involvement, leading to long periods of waiting.
- Prefers strategy games with meaningful player interaction over "multiplayer solitaire."
- Finds games more engaging when player decisions directly impact and affect other players.
- Appreciates asymmetric game design that forces interaction and prevents isolated optimization.
- Loves games with variable setup or asymmetric factions because they ensure each session feels fresh and not repetitive.
  - Example: In [[entities/root.md]], "Root's asymmetry creates genuine tension—you can't just optimize your own engine in isolation."

- Dislikes games where opening moves become formulaic and outcomes are predictable early on.
- Dislikes games that devolve into executing memorized sequences, losing the element of novel problem-solving over time.
- Dislikes cooperative games with simple mechanics that allow one player to solve the puzzle for the group, reducing other players to token-movers.
- Dislikes games that conclude very quickly (e.g., 20 minutes) with minimal player discussion, indicating a lack of meaningful interaction.
- Believes map variability adds a layer of freshness to games, enhancing replayability.
- Prefers games where each player has unique powers and a separate board, ensuring no single player can control all aspects of the game.
- Enjoys games that necessitate strategic discussions rather than allowing one person to dictate all the moves.
- Prefers games centered on discussions about probabilities and timing over solitary calculation of optimal moves.
- Appreciates cooperative games with distinct, asymmetric abilities that require coordination, preventing a single player from optimizing the entire game state.
- **High Agency & Low Downtime**: Believes player agency should be constant throughout the entire game; no one should feel like they're just waiting for their turn to matter.
- Values the tactile satisfaction of physical game components, like placing tiles.
- Appreciates beautiful, high-quality components.
- Prefers cooperative games that feel like solving a puzzle together, rather than one player dictating the optimal moves for everyone.
- Dislikes encounter systems that feel like random punishment rather than presenting meaningful choices.

## Game Organization and Storage
- Interested in methods for organizing board game inserts.
- Dislikes having too many small card games that take up significant shelf space.
- Prefers games with an easier setup to increase the frequency of play.
- Dislikes long setup times, as they kill the momentum for quick weeknight games.

- Considers some games as 'useful tools' for specific situations rather than for active personal enjoyment.

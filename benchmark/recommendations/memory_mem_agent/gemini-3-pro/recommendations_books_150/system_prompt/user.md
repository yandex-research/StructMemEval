# User Information

## Preferences
- **Coffee**: Oat milk latte with an extra shot (only when reading fantasy).
- **Tea**: Drink of choice when reading by the fireplace.
- **Environment**: Enjoys reading by the fireplace for "peak coziness".
- **Prose Style**: Finds dense, highly stylized prose (e.g., Hilary Mantel) beautiful but mentally exhausting. Dense historical detail and complex sentence structures break immersion.
- **Poetry**: Appreciates the art form but finds print collections overwhelming due to density. **Format Preference**: Strong preference for audio/listening to poets read their work; audio makes poetry accessible where print fails. **Key Insight**: Hearing rhythm and emphasis unlocks meaning. Uniquely, audio removes "performance anxiety," allowing the user to "receive" the poem rather than feeling the need to "solve" it. Validated by past experiences with Mary Oliver (print struggle) and Amanda Gorman (audio success). **Current Routine**: Finds daily enjoyment in *The Slowdown* podcast, marking the first time poetry has clicked as a regular habit. [[entities/the_slowdown.md]] [[entities/mary_oliver.md]] [[entities/amanda_gorman.md]]

## Interests
- Historical Fiction: Finds the genre challenging due to dense details, but worth the effort when executed well (e.g., *The Book Thief*). Specifically appreciates when history is "woven into character experience" rather than feeling like "history with characters attached" (critique of *Wolf Hall*, praise for *All the Light We Cannot See*).
- Reading (Fantasy): Captivated by world-building and the sense of discovery; finds literary fiction lacking in this regard.
- Magic Systems: Loves logical and consistent magic systems where costs and boundaries make victories feel earned. Specifically appreciates Brandon Sanderson's magic systems.
- Narrative Structure: Values intentional storytelling where the author clearly had a destination in mind; doesn't strictly need everything 'tied with a bow', but requires directedness. Dislikes fantasy series that end on cliffhangers or start strong but fizzle out.
- Series Commitment: Finds 10-book series overwhelming despite enjoying epic fantasy. **Anxiety**: Specifically anxious about unfinished series and investing in stories that remain incomplete.
- Character Development: Values character growth, change, and moral struggles over purely plot-driven narratives. Frustrated by characters who repeat the same mistakes without evolving.
- Romance Tropes: Enjoys "enemies-to-lovers" and "fake dating" when well-executed.
- Romance Depth: Values romance novels where characters have individual goals and struggles outside the relationship; believes a relationship should enhance individual journeys rather than replace them.
- Romance Fatigue: Enjoys the genre but gets frustrated when books blend together due to overused tropes and predictable endings. **Strategy**: Spaces out romance reads with other genres to keep appreciation fresh.
- Narrative Pet Peeves: Strongly dislikes the "miscommunication trope" and artificial drama driven by characters making stupid decisions. Cites **The Flatshare** as a positive example that avoids this.
- Horror (Psychological/Atmospheric): Loves horror novels that build psychological tension and atmospheric dread rather than relying on gore. **Specifics**: Wants **mounting paranoia** (e.g., checking house corners, feeling watched) and **no explicit violence**. Cites **The Haunting of Hill House** as the perfect example of this style. **Critique**: Believes modern horror often fails by showing too much; prefers the terror of the unseen over explicit monster reveals.

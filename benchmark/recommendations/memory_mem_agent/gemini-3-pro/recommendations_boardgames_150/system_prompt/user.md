# User Information

## Hobbies
- **Board Games**: Loves strategy games requiring deep thinking and long-term planning. Views each game as a unique puzzle shaped by player choices. Values agency and 'earned' victories over RNG. Core philosophy: "Player agency throughout the entire game. No one should feel like they're just waiting for their turn to matter." Prefers depth without excessive complexity or hours-long learning curves. Strong preference for iterative learning through play ('play three times to learn') rather than extensive rules study upfront. Specifically dislikes cooperative games prone to 'quarterbacking' where one player dictates moves for everyone; compares this passive experience to "watching a YouTube tutorial." Notes that simple mechanics often make the puzzle trivial for one player to solve alone, reducing others to merely "moving tokens on command." Described the feeling as "like a robot following instructions." Prefers strategy games with meaningful player interaction where decisions directly affect others, specifically defining engagement as moments when one's move forces opponents to adapt their strategy, rather than "multiplayer solitaire." Believes that "Every decision has consequences beyond my own board." [[entities/terraforming_mars.md]]
- **Replayability Preferences**: Prizes variable setup, map variability, and asymmetric factions (like in Root) to prevent repetition and keep the experience fresh.
- **Setup Preferences**: Values games with easier setup / lower table friction, noting that this directly increases play frequency ("Easier setup means more plays"). Specifically notes that long setup times kill momentum, especially for quick weeknight gaming sessions.
- **Twilight Imperium**: Interested but finds the rulebook intimidating. [[entities/twilight_imperium.md]]
- **Pandemic**: Disliked. Found it too mechanical, describing it as a "spreadsheet exercise.". [[entities/pandemic.md]]
- **Mechanics Dislike**: specifically dislikes "random punishment" mechanics (e.g., encounter systems that penalize players without meaningful choice).
- **Component Care**: Prefers using card sleeves to protect game components from oily fingerprints.
- **Collection State**: Is running out of shelf space due to an accumulation of many small card games.
- **Root**: Appreciates the tension created by its asymmetry, preventing isolated engine optimization. [[entities/root.md]]
- **Hive**: Lost appeal after about ten games. [[entities/hive.md]]
- **Forbidden Island**: Disliked even more than Pandemic due to quarterbacking issues. [[entities/forbidden_island.md]]
- **Spirit Island**: A rare positive cooperative experience. The combination of unique powers and separate boards ensures no single player can control the whole game, effectively solving the "quarterbacking" issue found in other coop games. [[entities/spirit_island.md]]
- **The Crew**: A refreshing cooperative exception. Works because hidden information enforces "genuine collaboration"; since no one sees all the cards, everyone must contribute meaningfully, preventing quarterbacking. [[entities/the_crew.md]]
- **Codenames**: Played with regular group, but becoming stale.
- **Party Games**: Generally finds them too shallow and repetitive after a few plays. Views them as "**useful tools for specific situations**" rather than games sought out for personal enjoyment, noting they lack strategic satisfaction. Explicitly prefers strategy games over party games when playing with serious gamers.
- **Abstract Games**: Appreciates the balance between simple rules and deep strategy found in games like Azul and Santorini; notes high replayability.
- **Azul**: Current evening wind-down game. [[entities/azul.md]]
- **Santorini**: Praised for simple rules and deep strategy. [[entities/santorini.md]]
- **Scythe**: Disappointing experience due to passive turns and combat system issues (kingmaking). Beautiful components did not compensate for the lack of agency when not the active player. [[entities/scythe.md]]
- **Combat Downtime**: Specifically frustrated by games where players spend entire rounds watching others resolve combat with no input.

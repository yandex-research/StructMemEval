## Preferences
- **Fantasy Genre**: Loves fantasy for the immersive feeling of a fully realized world with its own rules and history.
- **Magic Systems**: Strongly prefers "hard magic" systems with defined rules and costs; dislikes undefined magic functioning as deus ex machina.
- **Character Development**: Prioritizes internal character arc over plot events. Feels cheated if characters remain static; growth and change are essential requirements.
- **Atmosphere**: Finds thunderstorms to be perfect weather for reading.
- **Prose Style**: Appreciates writing where the prose itself feels "haunted" or atmospheric, citing Shirley Jackson as an example.
- **Beverage**: Oat milk latte is non-negotiable before reading. Adds an extra shot if reading Fantasy, and a double shot if reading Brandon Sanderson.
- **Series Completion**: Avoids starting unfinished sagas; prefers series with a guaranteed ending due to past disappointment with unfinished works. Explicitly stated a preference for multiple completed trilogies over a single unfinished decalogy.
- **Plot Direction**: Has low tolerance for wandering narratives which break their trust. Does not require a happy ending, but insists on feeling the author had a destination and plan from the start. Will abandon series (even deep in, e.g., Book 4) if the plot lacks clear direction.
- **Romance Tropes**: Strongly dislikes the "miscommunication trope", "forced chemistry", or situations where characters act unintelligently just to sustain drama ("idiot plot"). Conversely, enjoys "fake dating" and "enemies-to-lovers" tropes, provided the characters feel authentic and real. **Fantasy Specific**: Loves slow-burn romance growing naturally from shared trauma or goals. **Integration**: Believes romance must serve the plot; if it distracts, it fails, but if it enhances the narrative, it "sings".
- **Genre Pacing**: Loves the Romance genre but establishes a need to pace themselves; avoids reading more than three in a row to keep the stories distinct and maintain enjoyment.
- **Reading Strategy**: Sandwiches specific genres (likely Romance) between Fantasy and Horror to keep the reading experience fresh.
- **Horror Preference**: Prefers "dread over gore". Appreciates horror that scares without explicit violence, citing *The Haunting of Hill House*. Believes modern horror often neglects the power of silence, arguing it can be louder than screams.
- **Historical Settings**: Loves the setting but dislikes academic prose or overwhelming details. Loses interest if the focus shifts too much from the story to dense facts. Cites *The Book Thief* as the ideal balance where history is woven into emotion.
- **Poetry**: Appreciates not feeling pressured to intellectually "solve" or "get" a poem; values the experience over the analysis. Finds reading poetry on the page (print) feels like a test and makes them anxious, while spoken poetry (audio) flows like music and makes them curious.
